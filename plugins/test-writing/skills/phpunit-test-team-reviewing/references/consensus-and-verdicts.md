# Consensus & Verdicts — Adaptation Guide

Computed deterministically from the binding stances — Wave-1 peer stances in the review stage, updated by the Wave-3 defense fold and arbitration when the adversarial stage runs. The script (`mergeUnit` / `mergeFile` / `bucketFile` + arbitration) owns the computation; this is the consensus policy and its adaptation surface.

## Consensus policy

- **Finding identity** — `finding_id` = `${rule_id}|${methodId(method) || 'class-level'}`, and nothing else, where `methodId` strips a trailing `(...)` call suffix and surrounding whitespace from `method` (so `testFoo()` and `testFoo` are the same locator) and `'class-level'` stands in where `method` is absent or empty. Neither the line nor any fingerprint of the quoted code takes part: two reviewers reporting one defect at `:43` and `:47`, one quoting three lines and another thirty, or one quoting code while a third wrote only a `summary`, are ONE finding and pool their votes. Reviewing agents never emit a `finding_id` — the workflow stamps every finding as it arrives, and every later role quotes back the id it was handed. A finding is still required to carry `current` or `summary`; that is a contract check on the payload, not an identity input.
  - **Two costs are accepted, deliberately.** Two genuinely distinct defects citing the same rule in the same method merge into one record. (i) *Partial content loss, and a possible description/remediation mismatch.* Every remediation survives in `suggested_variants`, every position in `locations`, the deletion sets union, `enforce` takes the majority and `implies_src_change` ORs. But the descriptive fields (`summary`, `current`, `title`) follow the leading remediation's owner: in the per-unit merge they are that owner's alone, so the losing stance's are dropped and the record can describe defect A beside a `suggested_variants[1]` that fixes defect B. On the fallback paths (the file-level union and the defense promotion) it can invert — an owner whose schema does not require `current` borrows the paired record's, so defect B's remediation can render against defect A's quoted code. (ii) *Vote inflation.* The two reviewers read as two votes for the merged record and promote it to `kept` on a majority neither defect earned alone. `class-level` findings collapse hardest: one bucket per rule per file. The asymmetry is the argument: over-merging costs separation between two defects that are both still rendered, while fragmenting cost the finding entirely — a fingerprint made every reviewer's phrasing its own single-vote group, and single-vote groups are contested and excluded from the body. Do not reintroduce a similarity threshold to split them apart; a threshold reintroduces the fragmentation it is meant to bound, non-deterministically.
- **Per unit, per `finding_id`** — 3-of-3 unanimous → include (no dissent); 2-of-3 majority → include with a dissent annotation from the reviewer who omitted it; 1-of-3 minority → exclude from the body, record as contested.
- **A reviewer stance whose sub-skill guard refused is not a stance.** Each reviewing sub-skill runs its own deletion after-state guard over that reviewer's own findings, and on a refusal its contract mandates `status: FAILED` carrying the tool's verbatim error. The workflow's reviewer schema carries `status`/`reason` for exactly that; a `FAILED` stance is excluded from the consensus merge and its findings are not ingested — the guard refused over those findings, so they are the ones no reader should act on. Counting the stance would let a refusal masquerade as a reviewer who found nothing. A `FAILED` with no `reason` is a broken contract and stops the run: a refusal nobody can read is not a report. The file's status becomes `FAILED` with every refusal verbatim in `reason`, and the payload carries them across the stage boundary so the adversarial stage's final verdict cannot upgrade the file — no wave there re-runs the guard. No later wave backfills the slot: peer reconciliation runs one reconciler per *surviving* stance, never one per slot number, because a reconciler seeded with no findings of its own would adopt its peers' and return a vote with no independent review behind it — under the same reviewer label, so the merge would count it. Excluding these stances can drop a unit below the live-stance floor below; that fires the shard failure, correctly, and nothing may cancel it by manufacturing a replacement stance.
- **Lost reviewers fail the shard** — a unit whose merge sees fewer than **2** live stances throws, naming the unit and the stance count; the shard fails and is re-run. It never degrades quietly: with one stance every real finding is a 1-of-1 minority pushed into `contested`, and with zero the consensus is empty and the file renders as a clean `PASS` that no caller can distinguish from a correct one. Two is the threshold because consensus needs a second voice to agree or dissent with — at two stances a finding both report is `kept` and one only is visibly `contested`, which is honest; at one stance neither outcome exists. The wave circuit breaker does not cover this case (it never trips below `WAVE_NULL_MIN` agents, and a one-file Track-A wave is six). A reconciliation pass that itself came back partial does not trigger this: it replaces a unit's binding stances only when it produced at least 2 of them, and otherwise leaves the richer prior binding in place and logs the discarded pass.
- **Cross-track merge** — merge within each unit's 3 stances, then union per file by `finding_id`; a finding that reaches consensus in any unit wins over a contested one, and the two units' remediation and location lists are unioned rather than the loser's being dropped. The union runs unit by unit in one pass, so a finding that alternates between the kept and contested sides across units still lists its locations in the order the units met them, not grouped by which side each came from.
- **Enforce-level conflict** — take the majority enforce level, note the disagreement.
- **Remediation payload** — every distinct remediation the stances proposed survives. Duplicates collapse under whitespace normalization; the rest are ordered longest first in `suggested_variants`, and `suggested` is the first — the most complete one. `location`, `summary`, `current`, and `method` come from the record that supplied it, so `current` and `suggested` describe one change, and `locations` carries every location the stances reported, first seen first. Where that record carries no value of its own for one of those four fields — possible only for a payload whose schema does not require it, such as a defender's `re_adopted`/`adopted_new` entry — the field falls back to the record it was merged with rather than being dropped. *No value* means the field is absent, never that it is empty: a `current` of `""` is a finding that quoted no code and a `method` of `""` is the `class-level` locator `methodId` reads it as, so each stands as that record's own value and neither borrows another record's. `title` is the one field that never takes the paired record's value directly: it is the owner's own `title` when it has one, else a short form of whichever `summary` won the fallback above — so the title always describes the summary actually rendered, never the paired record's title. Unlike the other four fields, an owner's blank `title` (`""`) also counts as absent here and falls through to the summary-derived form. No stance's remediation is discarded at any stage.
- **Deletion accounting** — `deleted_methods` (the test methods a remediation removes entirely) and `removed_assertions` (`{assertion, covered_by_test}` pairs, `covered_by_test` naming the surviving test that still covers the assertion or the literal `none — coverage lost`) ride through every merge the way `suggested_variants` does, never following the winning variant's owner alone: `deleted_methods` unions across the merged stances, method names normalized by `methodId` so `testFoo()` and `testFoo` are one entry; `removed_assertions` concatenates in first-seen order with exact duplicates collapsed under whitespace normalization. The same at the file-level union, the defense-stage promotion, and the report and contested entries. `covered_by_test` deliberately avoids the name `covered_by`, which the result shape already uses for the SUT-coverage map.
- **Status** — `FAILED` (this file collected a deletion after-state guard refusal); else `ISSUES_FOUND` (1+ must-fix in any file); else `NEEDS_ATTENTION` (should-fix warnings, or consistency findings); else `PASS`. `FAILED` outranks every other status, at file and run level alike, a baseline-fail `ISSUES_FOUND` included. **`informational` never raises status.** A `consider`-level finding, the INTEGRATION-008 placement hint, and the workflow's own "split this test class" entry all ride the informational channel and leave the file at `PASS` — the reviewing sub-skills define that channel as status-neutral, and escalating it made one clean integration test `PASS` to `phpunit-integration-test-reviewing` and `NEEDS_ATTENTION` to the team review.

## What you can adapt

- The **vote thresholds** (3-of-3 / 2-of-3 / 1-of-3).
- The **variant ordering** in `suggested_variants` (longest first) — never whether the non-leading variants are kept: dropping one loses a remediation nothing downstream can recover.
- The **live-stance floor** (currently 2) below which a unit fails the shard — raise it to demand a full panel, never lower it to 1 or 0, which is the fail-open this floor replaced.
- **Arbitration** (adversarial stage) — contested findings are arbitrated up to the preset's **hard caps**: `arbFile` per file, then `arbMax` per run, candidates sorted must-fix-first at both levels so the caps trim the lowest-severity tail first; every trimmed finding stays contested and visible. An arbitrated contested **must-fix** gets **3 adversary-tier arbiters** (opus on the default model combo) and a majority verdict: ≥ 2 `confirmed` → include (+ `corrected_enforce`); ≥ 2 `refuted` → exclude/contested; no majority → **keep**, marked `arbitration: split` (a possibly-real must-fix is never silently dropped). should-fix / consider keep a **single** body-tier arbiter: `confirmed` → include; `refuted` → exclude/contested; `uncertain` → keep contested.

## Already handled — do not re-adapt

- **Adversary-impact tagging** — every finding carries `unchanged` / `defended` / `overturned` / `resurrected` / `introduced` (all `unchanged` when the adversarial stage was skipped at the gate).
- **Cross-file findings** — the dedicated cross-file agent (the `signals` stage) is their sole producer (fingerprint input → `consistency[]` warnings; counts toward `NEEDS_ATTENTION`, never `ISSUES_FOUND`).
- **"Split this test class"** — an `L > C` file emits a first-class informational entry; its method-shard and digest findings still merge.
- **Synthesized rule ids** — `TEAM-SPLIT` is emitted by this workflow, is not a catalog rule, and can never be fetched (a run reaches no MCP tool). Nothing validates a `rule_id` against the catalog, so they flow through identity, merge and rendering like any other id. Two places account for them explicitly: the agent guardrails name them and tell an agent their absence from the `## RULES` block is correct rather than a gap to fill, and arbitration skips a contested finding that cites one — a synthesized rule has no detection algorithm to arbitrate against, so the finding stays contested and visible instead of an arbiter inventing a verdict against an empty rule block. Add an id to `SYNTHETIC_RULE_IDS` when you add a synthesized rule; do not add a catalog lookup for one.
- **Coverage overlaps & placement flags** — the cross-cutting SUT-coverage map (`coverage_overlap[]`) and the integration-to-unit placement flags (`placement_flags[]`) are computed deterministically by the skill's merge step from the manifest + the merged INTEGRATION-008 consensus signal. They are **informational**: they never raise status to `ISSUES_FOUND`, form their own finding class separate from quality findings, and only point at the standalone `phpunit-integration-to-unit-migrating` skill.
- **Per-finding `method` / `branch_touched` / `implies_src_change`** — every finding carries the method it occurs in (the stable locator; `class-level` for structural), a `branch_touched` flag computed post-merge from the manifest's `changed_methods` on diff-scoped runs (`null` otherwise), and `implies_src_change` (true when **any** clustered stance flagged it — the fix needs a production change). All three are informational metadata: none change consensus or status. Rendered shape in report-format.md.
- **Defense-stage promotion identity** — an adoption or re-adoption resolves its `finding_id` against the record that already carries it, searched in one file's scope only: the consensus entry (kept or contested), then the withdrawn original the review stage persisted beside the withdrawal in `adversarial_input.withdrawn_originals` (a finding every peer withdrew survives in neither set, so a resurrection of it would otherwise resolve to nothing; the stored original is the finding's most-merged state at the withdrawal — the Wave-0 stances that reported it unioned with the reconciler stances that maintained it afterwards, so a remediation a peer pass first proposed and a later pass withdrew is restored with it; where two units of one file both withdrew the id, both units' contributors merge into one stored original rather than the first unit's standing alone), then the red team's own `new_findings` entry. An id resolving to none of them is a broken back-reference and stops the run. `finding_id` and `rule_id` are that record's and only that record's, so a defender cannot re-label the finding it names. The remaining fields follow the remediation as everywhere else: `method`, `location`, `summary`, and `current` come from whichever record supplied `suggested_variants[0]` — the resolved original, or the defender whose remediation leads — falling back to the original for any field that record does not carry at all, so a defender omitting `method` still cannot downgrade a method-level finding to `class-level`. `title` is the one field that never takes the paired record's value directly: it is the owner's own `title` when it has one, else a short form of whichever `summary` won the fallback above — so the title always describes the summary actually rendered, never the paired record's title. `enforce` is the defenders' majority over the voting entries: a defender that lists one finding twice is one voice in that tally, and the repeat entry's remediation and location still merge in — at the point the entry was seen, not behind the last defender's, so `locations` stays first-seen across defenders. The promoted record then reconciles against `kept`/`contested` by that same id — merged into whichever set already carries it, spliced out of `contested` when the merge lands in `kept` — instead of being appended as a fresh duplicate.
- **Adversary new findings are indexed per file** — `finding_id` embeds no path, so the red team's `new_findings` are keyed by the file they were raised against; one id on two files names two defects and never cross-resolves. Two lens adversaries raising the same id on one file are one finding, their remediations unioned rather than the later one overwriting the earlier.
